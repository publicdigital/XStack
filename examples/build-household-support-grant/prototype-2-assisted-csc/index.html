Help someone apply for the Household Support Grant

The Household Support Grant pays K 1,500 a month to households that need help with money.

You run this service on the CSC's certified device. The citizen sits beside you. Read the blue boxes out loud to them.

Before you start

  • The citizen is here in person, or a person acting for them is.
  • You use your own name on this device. It is recorded on every page.
  • You never ask for a PIN. Not for a wallet, not for a bank.
Does the citizen have a KestriaID? A card or a number. If they are not sure, choose No. We will use another way.

Are you a village digital champion?

You can help someone get ready and book a visit. You cannot sign anyone in or look up their records. A CSC officer does that.

Who is here with you?

 This decides whose fingerprint you check, and what can be changed.

Who is acting for the household?

Someone acting for a household can apply. They cannot change where the money is paid.

    Question map

    • Name: known (KestriaID claims). No KestriaID: ask.
    • District: known (National Population Register), shown as district only. Out of date or missing: ask, then flag for a survey.
    • Household members, support already got: known (National Social Registry via KesLink). No record: book a survey visit.
    • Eligibility: derived. The band is never shown. Only the outcome.
    • Where we pay: known (KesPay lookup, provider and last 4 digits). Changing it needs level 3.
    • Changes, who is paid, safe contact number, declaration: ask.
    • Not pulled or asked: date of birth, sex, KUIN, TIN, vehicle, tax, land, health, disability.

    Assumptions

    • Verify with users Citizens agree to a fingerprint check at the counter, and believe that saying no costs them nothing.
    • Verify with policy A fingerprint is sensitive data (Data Protection Act 2022, s.15). We assume consent (s.12) read aloud is enough, and that the print is not kept. Check both.
    • Known gap The DPI mock always reports level "substantial". The fingerprint page is our own simulated screen and sets level 3 in page code. The mock's sign-in is not tied to it.
    • Known gap The mock signs in one person. For "acting for someone", the researcher picks the household holder's persona. A real flow has two people: the one at the desk (level 3) and the household.
    • Verify with policy An authorised intermediary record at a CSC lets someone act for a person. What it covers is not defined. We assume it covers applying, not changing the payout.
    • Verify with policy The "check their card instead" route counts as level 2 (assumed). The profile defines no level for an officer's visual card check.
    • Known gap Data use is a notice (s.13 public task), but the mock's data screen always asks "Can we get your details?". We show a notice page first and still call the mock. "No" on either becomes manual answers and a survey visit.
    • Verify with policy A citizen can object to an s.13 lookup at the counter. We assume "no" means a survey visit, not a refusal of service.
    • Known gap Payouts: the mock has no payout contract (payments.create takes money in), so it is not called. The payout account page is an ordinary service page with fake data.
    • Verify with platform The Treasury disbursement lookup returns only the provider and last 4 digits. It has no published spec (Tier 3).
    • Verify with platform How a payout change is made at a CSC is not defined. The profile says citizens link accounts at their provider. We let the officer record a mobile money number or cash at a post office.
    • Known gap The phone number on record is not in the mock's claims or attributes. "Text the old number" cannot show a number. We ask for a safe contact number instead.
    • Verify with platform When someone other than the applicant is paid, we assume the money goes to the account linked to their KestriaID. Nothing in the profile says how that is found.
    • Verify with department The grant is K 1,500 a month. A complete application takes about 4 weeks. A survey visit takes 8 to 10 weeks.
    • Verify with policy Demo eligibility: the household is in the lowest bands and gets no other SPA cash programme. The band and score are never shown. A household that looks higher gets a visit, not a no.
    • Known gap District, member names and the band are invented in a separate PERSONA_EXTRAS object, not the mock. The mock's address is one full-address string, so we show the district from the extras.
    • Verify with users People are happy for household names to be read aloud and shown at a counter where others can see or hear. Officer screen angle and queue privacy need testing.
    • Verify with users Officers will read the blue boxes in full. We think they will shorten them in a busy CSC.
    • Verify with department Officers can interpret in Vell and Sora, or an interpreter is available. The profile only mentions interpreters for research.
    • Known gap The language switcher shows a notice for Kestrian, Vell and Sora. The profile has no text in those languages. "Language wanted" is a separate question, for the citizen.
    • Verify with platform GovNotify sends the text in the chosen language, and the reply "STATUS" shows progress. The profile gives *999# and the free line only.
    • Verify with department The slip is printed on the CSC printer. It says nothing about the result, only the reference and next steps, because paper is shared as well as phones.
    • Verify with policy A declaration read aloud and agreed to by the officer's record is valid (Electronic Transactions Act 2020, s.6 and s.8). The penalty sentence is invented.
    • Verify with department References are HSG- plus 7 digits. The booking reference (BK- plus 6 digits) is invented.
    • Known gap Looking up a household by household number (without a KUIN) is not in the profile. The prototype records the number and goes to a survey. It looks nothing up.
    • Verify with policy The village committee still confirms households. We record a name only. Who must confirm, and how, is not defined.
    • Verify with department Digital champions use this same tool to prepare and refer only. They see no records. Collecting a first name, village and safe phone number needs a lawful basis and a retention rule.
    • Known gap Mobile CSC days, booking slots and "nearest CSC from district" do not exist in the profile. All dates here are made up.
    • Verify with data The rainy season (March to June, roads cut in April and May in the Northern Plains and parts of the Lake Region) drives the choice of a mobile CSC day.
    • Known gap The officer, the champion and the Dunmar CSC are invented. The profile has no CSC list or staff names.
    • Verify with policy The audit log shows what is recorded (officer, CSC, acting for, ID method, lookup consent ID). The real fields and retention are not defined.
    • Known gap The confirmation page has no Back link on purpose. It stops a second submit. Every other page except Start has one.
    • Built in Never ask for a PIN, a full account number or a KUIN. The pages say "We will never ask for your PIN" on the payout page and the confirmation page.
    • Built in No raw score or band is shown. The slip and the text message say nothing about the result, and never say "you qualify".
    • Built in Fingerprint consent is a separate step. A no goes to a card check, and the citizen keeps the service. Payout changes need level 3 and a person present for themselves.
    • Built in Every route works without a KUIN. The officer's name and CSC are on every page. "Acting for" is logged. Someone acting for a household cannot change the payout account.
