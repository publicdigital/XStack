# Build – Apply for the Household Support Grant

> **Kestria profile example. Kestria is a fictional country.** This build uses `profiles/kestria/` to show what xstack does with a government running on MOSIP and eSignet, Mojaloop and X-Road. It also tests the profile: see `profile-findings.md`.

- **Generated by:** the `xstack:brief-to-prototypes` skill, via `/xstack:build`
- **Brief source:** `brief.md`. This is a synthetic demo brief made from the profile's world bible, because Kestria has no real discovery.
- **Date:** 2026-10-23
- **Status:** three candidate prototypes, clicked through end to end in a browser. Not tested with users, because none exist.

> These prototypes are throwaway alpha. Every person, number and institution in them is fictional.

## The three hypotheses

| # | Name | Hypothesis | Strongest for | Weakest for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Once-only, phone-first** (`prototype-1-once-only/`) | The citizen signs in with KestriaID. We pull their household, programmes and payout account, and they confirm rather than retype. They see the outcome before applying. | Smartphone owners already in the registry whose KestriaID phone number works | Shared phones, people not in the registry |
| 2 | **Assisted at a Citizen Service Centre** (`prototype-2-assisted-csc/`) | A CSC officer runs the service, and the citizen signs in by fingerprint after a separate consent step. Village champions prepare and refer. Not being in the registry becomes a booked survey visit. | No smartphone, old phone number, low literacy, Vell and Sora speakers, no KUIN | People far from a CSC, especially in the rainy season |
| 3 | **Household-first** (`prototype-3-household-first/`) | Any adult tells SPA what's changed in the household and who should get the money. The default outcome is a reassessment or visit. | Households whose record is out of date. The person who looks after the money. | People who just want a yes or no |

The service designer replaced the original third idea, a proactive SMS offer. It was the same bet as Prototype 1 with a different way in, it gave only the lowest level of sign-in, and it left out households not in the registry. Why each decision was made is in `build-spec.md`.

## How to open them

Open any `index.html` in a browser. There's no server and nothing is loaded from the internet. When KestriaID asks who to sign in as, pick a test person – each one runs a different scenario (see `test-plan.md`). Press "Show assumptions" in the black xstack banner to see what the prototype is assuming.

All three have the same chrome, so the test is about the journey:
- a "Kestria is a fictional country" banner
- the GovKestria header with a language switcher
- the alpha banner
- the xstack banner
- the footer with the CSC finder

Iterations 1 and 2 use the xstack neutral style, because Kestria had no design system then. **Iteration 3 uses the GovKestria design system** (`profiles/kestria/design-system/`) – see below. Iteration 3 loads Noto Sans from Google Fonts, and falls back to Arial offline.

## Iteration 4 (2026-10-23) – Kestrian

Each prototype now has `iteration-4/` with `index.html`, `CHANGES.md` and `assumptions.md`. The feedback is `feedback-round-3.md`: Kestria now has a described language, Kestrian (*kestrin*), in `profiles/kestria/kestrian.md`.

**The language switcher works for Kestrian.**
- **What switches:** every screen, error and page title, and the simulated KestriaID, KesLink and GovPay screens.
- **Switching back** restores the original English exactly.
- **The links:**
  - In P1, the one link reads **Kestrin** or **English** – the other language, named in that language.
  - In P2 and P3, "Kestrin" and "English" both appear, and `aria-current` marks the active one.
  - Vell and Sora still show a notice.
- **Not changed:** the journeys, the data, the GovKestria styling and the page's own scripts are the same as iteration 3.

**Kestrian is invented.** Nobody speaks it, so the prototypes can show the switcher and the layouts working, but they're no evidence about real people reading in their own language.

**How it's built:**
- **`kestrin/strings.json`:** 1,381 English → Kestrian entries, shared by all three prototypes. Patterns use `<x>` for text the page fills in at runtime. New words are in the Lexicon in `kestrian.md`.
- **`kestrin/kestrin.js`:** the switcher.
  - It translates text on the page, and keeps translating as the page changes.
  - It never touches the xstack banner, the assumptions panel or fake data.
  - `xstackKestrin.missing()` in the browser console lists anything still in English.
  - Adding `#kestrin` to the address should start in Kestrian. That's untested here.

**What was checked:**
- `node --check` passes, and the page's own scripts are identical to iteration 3.
- **In a browser, in Kestrian:**
  - **Journeys to confirmation:**
    - P1 as Ines, Desa, Mirel and Talin
    - P2 as Aru, as Anaïs with Mina acting for her, and as Mirel
    - P3 as Tessa, Talin (new baby) and Ines
  - every page shown, every question submitted empty to show its errors, and each simulated platform screen opened

  Nothing was left in English apart from names, codes and brands.
- Switching back restored the English.
- No page scrolls sideways at 320px in Kestrian.

**Not checked:** a screen reader, zoom, and other routes.

**Costs and findings:**
- **Size:** the strings add about 145 KB per prototype (40 KB gzipped), which is over `DESIGN.md`'s 100 KB budget. A real service would load one language at a time.
- **Length:** long Kestrian sentences turned out about as long as the English, not 20–30% longer, and `kestrian.md` now says so.
- **An existing P3 bug:** P3 repeats "Error:" in the page title on each failed submit. That was already in iteration 3, in English.
